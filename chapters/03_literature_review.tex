\section{Literature Review}

\subsection{Overview of Social Media Misinformation Detection}

Research into social media misinformation detection has developed across three main directions: text-based content modelling, multimodal content analysis, and social-context or propagation-based modelling. Earlier approaches commonly treated misinformation detection as a supervised classification task based on textual cues, where models classified posts, articles, or claims as authentic or inauthentic using linguistic and semantic features \citep{Wang2018,Song2021,truica2024danes}. These approaches remain important because they provide strong and relatively efficient baselines.

However, social media misinformation is no longer communicated only through text. Inauthentic content frequently combines written claims with images, memes, screenshots, edited visual material, comments, reposts, user engagement, and propagation patterns. This means that a model relying only on text may miss important visual or social signals \citep{Wang2018,Nakamura2020,Qian2021}. Recent survey work also shows that misinformation detection has moved beyond isolated text classification toward broader models that consider content, source, propagation, and social relationships \citep{emil2025autofakenews,phan2023graphsurvey,gong2023fakenewsdetectiongraphbased}.

This broader direction motivates the proposed MACE framework. Existing multimodal models show that text and images are useful together, while DANES and GETAE show that social-context modelling can improve detection \citep{Qian2021,truica2024danes,GETAE2025}. However, there is still limited work that performs a controlled replacement of the text-only content branch inside a DANES/GETAE-style propagation ensemble while keeping the social branch, fusion mechanism, and classifier largely unchanged. This creates the central gap addressed by this research.

\subsection{Text-Only and Social-Context Detection Approaches}

Text-only content-based approaches remain a useful starting point because they are simpler, easier to reproduce, and computationally cheaper than multimodal systems. They avoid the additional requirements of image collection, image preprocessing, visual feature extraction, and multimodal fusion. However, their limitation is increasingly significant because visual misinformation is common in social media environments. \citet{yang2023facebook} show that image-based political misinformation is a substantial part of online misinformation, while \citet{hameleers2020} show that multimodal disinformation can influence credibility judgements. These findings suggest that text-only models structurally exclude an important detection signal.

This limitation also applies to DANES and GETAE. Both models are strong context-aware baselines because they combine textual content with social-context or propagation information. DANES uses a text-based content branch and a social-context branch, while GETAE extends this idea using graph information and propagation-enhanced embeddings \citep{truica2024danes,GETAE2025}. Their dual-branch structure is valuable because it separates content evidence from social-context evidence before classification. However, the content branch remains text-only, meaning that visual misinformation is not explicitly modelled.

Social-context approaches are important because misinformation differs not only in what it says, but also in how it spreads. Prior work shows that diffusion structures, user interactions, temporal patterns, and propagation graphs can provide useful evidence for fake news detection \citep{Vosoughi2018,Song2021,Min2022,Zhu24}. However, these methods depend heavily on the availability and quality of interaction data. In early detection settings, a post may not yet have enough comments, reposts, or propagation traces. In other cases, deleted posts, missing users, or sparse engagement data can weaken the social graph. This means that dataset suitability is central to whether context-aware models can be applied reliably.

\subsection{Multimodal Content-Based Detection Approaches}

Multimodal misinformation detection addresses the limitations of text-only modelling by combining textual and visual features. EANN showed that combining text and images can improve detection compared with unimodal approaches \citep{Wang2018}. Fakeddit further supported this direction by providing a large-scale multimodal benchmark for evaluating fake news categories \citep{Nakamura2020}. Later models focused not only on adding image features, but on how text and images should be fused. MCNN modelled text-image consistency, HMCAN introduced hierarchical multimodal contextual attention, and MPFN demonstrated the value of progressive multimodal fusion \citep{Xue2021,Qian2021,Jing2023}.

For MACE, HMCAN is especially relevant because it is selected as the multimodal content branch. HMCAN uses BERT for textual features and ResNet-50 for visual features, producing a fixed-length multimodal post representation that can replace the original text-only content embedding in a DANES/GETAE-style ensemble \citep{Devlin2019,He2016,Qian2021}. This makes it suitable for the proposed controlled substitution design.

Although newer visual encoders such as ViT and CLIP may offer stronger image-text alignment, MACE does not aim to optimise the visual encoder itself \citep{Dosovitskiy2021,Radford2021}. Instead, the purpose is to test whether replacing the original text-only content branch with an HMCAN-based multimodal branch improves detection while the rest of the ensemble remains controlled. This makes HMCAN a practical choice because it balances multimodal capability, reproducibility, and compatibility with the proposed experimental design.

\subsection{Integrating Multimodal Content and Social Context}
\label{sec:integrating}

Recent research increasingly combines multimodal content with social-context information. EFND combines semantic, visual, and socially augmented features \citep{Nadeem2023}. Wang et al.'s C2DCMTN integrates multimodal content with comment-based context \citep{Wang2024}. DPSG combines multimodal features with dynamic propagation social graphs \citep{jing2025}. Dellys et al. examine the role of social context in multimodal fake news detection \citep{Dellys2025}. Other recent models, including TMEF-BI, ESDGFN, and LDPG, further show the value of combining multimodal content with behaviour, stance, or dynamic graph information \citep{Chen2025,Sun25,Ji2026LDPG}.

These studies confirm that multimodal content and social context can be complementary. However, they also reveal the main methodological gap. Most integrated models change several components at the same time, such as the text encoder, visual encoder, graph construction, fusion strategy, and classifier. As a result, it is difficult to determine whether performance improvements come from multimodal content, social-context modelling, fusion design, or another architectural change.

A detailed comparison of these related multimodal-social models is provided in Appendix~\ref{sec:litreviewsummary}. Overall, the reviewed models differ substantially in their encoder choices, fusion strategies, and social-context representations. This supports the need for a controlled substitution study rather than only another full-model comparison. MACE is therefore positioned differently: it keeps the DANES/GETAE-style social-context branch, fusion mechanism, and classifier as stable as possible, while replacing only the text-only content branch with an HMCAN-based multimodal branch.

This distinction is important because full-model comparisons alone are not sufficient to explain why a model improves. If a new model changes the content encoder, social graph construction, fusion mechanism, and classifier at the same time, then any performance gain cannot be clearly attributed to one design choice. For the proposed study, the key question is narrower and more controlled: whether the content branch in a strong context-aware backbone can be improved by replacing text-only representation with multimodal representation. This makes MACE less of a completely new end-to-end architecture and more of a controlled architectural evaluation, where the main variable of interest is the content-branch substitution.

This controlled substitution design directly supports RQ1 by testing whether multimodal replacement improves performance, and RQ2 by enabling ablation-based analysis of content and social-context contributions.

\subsection{Computational Cost and Dataset Selection Challenges}

Although multimodal models can improve detection, they also introduce additional computational cost. A text-only model mainly processes text, while an HMCAN-style pipeline requires both a text encoder and a visual encoder, as well as additional preprocessing and fusion operations \citep{Qian2021,Devlin2019,He2016}. Many fake news detection studies report accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, but fewer report training time, inference time, memory usage, or storage requirements. This limits the practical interpretation of results because a model with higher accuracy may be difficult to deploy at scale. For this reason, MACE treats computational cost as part of the research problem and proposes to report training time, inference time, peak memory usage, storage requirements, and hardware configuration.

Dataset selection is also a major challenge. MACE requires both multimodal content and social or propagation information, but many datasets do not provide all of these signals together. Twitter15 and Twitter16 are useful for Stage 1 replication because they support DANES/GETAE-style propagation modelling, but they are limited for full multimodal evaluation. FakeNewsNet and MuMin are more relevant because they include richer multimodal and social-context information, but they also introduce issues such as missing media, broken image links, sparse social traces, multilingual data, class imbalance, and large graph size \citep{shu2020fakenewsnet,mumindataset}.

This dataset issue is especially important because MACE requires alignment between three types of evidence: textual content, image content, and social-context structure. A dataset may be suitable for multimodal content detection but unsuitable for DANES/GETAE-style propagation modelling if it lacks usable interaction graphs. Conversely, a dataset may contain strong propagation traces but limited image availability, making it unsuitable for evaluating the multimodal content branch. Therefore, Stage 2 dataset adaptation is not only a preprocessing step, but a necessary validity check before interpreting model performance.

Recent dataset survey work confirms that only a limited number of multimodal fake news datasets provide text, image, and network modalities together \citep{lv2025multimodal}. Cross-domain research also shows that fake news detection performance can degrade when models are transferred across datasets, and that the value of text and network features varies by domain \citep{silva2021embracing}. Therefore, dataset choice cannot be treated as a neutral implementation detail. MACE addresses this through Stage 2 dataset adaptation, where candidate datasets are assessed for image availability, graph suitability, label compatibility, class balance, missing data, memory requirements, and storage requirements before final evaluation.

\subsection{Research Gap and Positioning of the Proposed Study}

The reviewed literature shows that misinformation detection has advanced through text-only modelling, multimodal content learning, social-context modelling, and integrated multimodal-social frameworks. However, a clear gap remains in the controlled integration of multimodal content into established propagation-based ensemble architectures.

Text-only models provide useful baselines but miss visual misinformation. Multimodal models improve text-image representation but often exclude structured social context. Context-aware models such as DANES and GETAE incorporate propagation and social signals, but their content branches remain text-only. Recent multimodal-social models demonstrate the value of combining content and context, but they often change several architectural components at once, making it difficult to isolate the specific source of improvement \citep{Nadeem2023,jing2025,Dellys2025,Chen2025,Sun25,Ji2026LDPG}.

The novelty of MACE is therefore the controlled substitution of the original DANES/GETAE text-only content branch with an HMCAN-based multimodal branch while retaining the original social-context branch, fusion mechanism, and classifier as far as possible \citep{truica2024danes,GETAE2025,Qian2021}. This design allows performance changes to be attributed more directly to multimodal content substitution rather than to unrelated architectural changes. The study also extends prior work by treating computational cost and dataset suitability as explicit research concerns. Therefore, MACE contributes both architecturally and methodologically to context-aware multimodal misinformation detection.
