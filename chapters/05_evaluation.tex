\section{Evaluation}

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}

Dataset selection for MACE evaluation proceeds in two stages. The candidate pool is drawn from \citet{lv2025multimodal}, a taxonomy of eighteen multimodal fake news datasets classified by modality availability, classification structure, and source platform.

In the first stage screening, candidate datasets must include: (1) a text modality; (2) a network or propagation modality to support graph-based social context modelling; and (3) a binary or near-binary authenticity classification structure compatible with the MACE output layer. The datasets meeting these criteria are summarised in Appendix~\ref{sec:computeexperiment}, Table~\ref{tab:stage1_candidates}.

In the second stage (Section~\ref{sec:dataset_adaptation}), a detailed adaptation and feasibility assessment determines suitability for Stage 3 experiments; this assessment directly addresses RQ3.

\subsection{Dataset Adaptation}
\label{sec:dataset_adaptation}

All Stage~2 candidate datasets will undergo adaptation to verify suitability for both branches of the
MACE architecture; missing or incomplete modalities degrade feature extraction and
fusion~\citep{Baltrusaitis2019}. Candidates are evaluated against the following criteria.

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Text Availability}. Textual content is processed by HMCAN's BERT
    encoder~\citep{Qian2021}. Adaptation will assess availability and completeness to confirm
    sufficient records for multimodal representation learning and classification~\citep{ENGLMEIER2021149}.

    \item \textbf{Image Availability}. Images are processed by HMCAN's ResNet-50
    encoder~\citep{Qian2021}. Adaptation will measure the proportion of records with accessible images
    and quantify missing or broken links, determining where dummy placeholders are required to preserve
    data alignment. Datasets with low image availability will still be considered, but the implications
    for the multimodal branch evaluation will be reported as RQ3 findings~\citep{Dellys2025}.

    \item \textbf{Social-Context Availability}. MACE retains the graph-based social-context branches
    of DANES and GETAE~\citep{truica2024danes, GETAE2025, Song2021}, so candidate datasets must provide
    sufficient user-level metadata, interaction logs, propagation traces, and network connectivity to
    support graph construction.

    \item \textbf{Label Mapping and Class Distribution}. Source labels
    (e.g., real/fake, rumor/non-rumor/unverified) will be mapped to a unified binary classification
    structure, and class distribution and imbalance measured across
    candidates~\citep{Xue2021, mumindataset}. For MR2, which provides three classes
    (rumor, non-rumor, unverified), a specific label mapping strategy will be defined and reported.

    \item \textbf{Language and Domain Compatibility}. Candidate datasets collected from non-English
    platforms (Weibo, CHECKED) require assessment of language compatibility with the BERT-based text
    encoder used in HMCAN, which was pre-trained predominantly on English-language corpora. Domain-
    specific datasets (CoAID, ReCOVery) will be assessed for whether their topical focus on COVID-19
    misinformation limits the generalisability of results to the broader misinformation detection
    problem addressed by MACE.

\end{itemize}

Stage~2 will produce subset statistics for each dataset, distinguishing the published dataset from the
usable subset and confirming that the adapted data can support the controlled ablation conditions
required by RQ1 and RQ2.

\subsection{Benchmarking}

MACE results will be compared against baselines across three categories:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Unimodal with social-context modelling}, consisting of DANES \citep{truica2024danes}
    and GETAE \citep{GETAE2025}. These models serve as the primary baseline for combining text or graph
    neural networks with social context features.

    \item \textbf{Multimodal without social-context modelling}, consisting of HMCAN \citep{Qian2021}.
    As a sub-component of MACE, the standalone performance of this model is considered as a comparator
    during ablation experiments.

    \item \textbf{Multimodal with social-context modelling}, consisting of DPSG \citep{jing2025} and
    the multimodal fusion framework of \citet{Dellys2025}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

Based on the methodology described in Section~4, \textbf{Stage 1} forms the baseline for evaluation. \textbf{Stage 2} assesses candidate datasets based on dataset adaptation metrics described in Section~\ref{sec:dataset_adaptation} and these results will be reported as part of the RQ3 findings. 

\textbf{Stage 3} evaluates the MACE framework across the experimental conditions defined in
Section~4, using standard classification metrics consistent with DANES~\citep{truica2024danes},
GETAE~\citep{GETAE2025}, and HMCAN~\citep{Qian2021, su_wan_liu_huang_2020}:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Accuracy}, measures the number of correct classifications divided by the total number
    of classifications.

    \item \textbf{Recall}, measures the number of inauthentic posts correctly classified out of all
    actual inauthentic posts (true positive rate).

    \item \textbf{Precision}, measures the number of inauthentic posts correctly classified out of all
    posts classified as inauthentic by the model.

    \item \textbf{F1 Score}, the harmonic mean of recall and precision, balancing false positives and
    false negatives equally.
\end{itemize}

F1 score will be used as the primary comparison metric across models and datasets, as it captures
the balance of detection capability (recall) and reliability (precision). All metrics are evaluated
during Stage~3 across Conditions A--D, enabling consistent comparison between the baseline and the
modified model and measuring the contribution of multimodal content and social context features.

Class imbalance is a known issue in misinformation datasets, often arising from the prevalence of false
claims in fact-checking sources~\citep{mumindataset}. To mitigate the impact on evaluation, F1-score
is prioritised over accuracy, and macro-averaged metrics are reported to ensure balanced consideration
of both classes.

To ensure that observed performance differences between models are not due to random variation,
statistical significance testing will be conducted~\citep{stat_test}. For pairwise model comparisons
on classification outputs, McNemar's test~\citep{McNemar_1947} will be used to assess differences in
prediction distributions on the same dataset. For experiments involving repeated runs or
cross-validation, a paired t-test will be applied across folds to evaluate whether differences in
performance metrics (e.g., F1-score) are statistically significant. A significance level of
$\alpha = 0.05$ will be used. Performance will be reported as mean and standard deviation across runs.

\subsection{Computational Efficiency Metrics}

In addition to classification performance, computational efficiency will be evaluated to determine the
practical cost of introducing multimodal content processing into the DANES and GETAE frameworks. The
following dimensions are reported following \citet{Justus2018}.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training Time}, measured as total wall-clock time per experimental condition,
    decomposed by batch processing time and total iterations per epoch.

    \item \textbf{Inference Time}, measured as the average prediction time per sample during testing.

    \item \textbf{Peak GPU Memory Usage}, measured as the maximum GPU memory consumed or allocated
    during model training and inference.

    \item \textbf{Peak System RAM Usage}, measured during graph construction and model execution.

    \item \textbf{Storage Requirements}, measured as the total storage required for model checkpoints,
    cached text or image embeddings, and processed graph structural data.

\end{itemize}

These metrics will directly support the evaluation of RQ1 by quantifying the computational cost
associated with substituting the original text-only branch with a multimodal content branch. To ensure
a systematic comparison, these metrics will be logged using the evaluation template structured in
Appendix~\ref{sec:computeexperiment}, Table~\ref{tab:efficiency_matrix}.
