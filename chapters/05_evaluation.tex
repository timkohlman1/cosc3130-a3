\section{Evaluation}

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}

Dataset selection for MACE evaluation proceeds in two stages. In the first stage, candidate datasets
are screened against basic acceptance criteria derived from the architectural requirements of the MACE
framework. In the second stage (Section~\ref{sec:dataset_adaptation}), datasets that pass the
first-stage screen undergo detailed adaptation and feasibility assessment to determine suitability for
Stage 3 experiments; this assessment directly addresses RQ3.

To establish the first-stage candidate pool, we draw on the survey by \citet{lv2025multimodal}, which
catalogues eighteen multimodal fake news detection datasets published between 2017 and 2024, classifying
each by modality availability (text, image, network, video), classification structure, and data source.
The basic acceptance criteria applied at this stage are: (1) the dataset must include a text modality;
(2) the dataset must include a network or propagation modality to support graph-based social context
modelling, as this is the binding architectural requirement for the DANES/GETAE-style social context
branch; and (3) the dataset must use a binary or near-binary authenticity classification structure
compatible with the MACE output layer. Image availability is not applied as a hard exclusion criterion
at this stage, as HMCAN supports dummy image placeholders for posts without attached
images~\citep{Qian2021}; however, image availability rate is recorded and carried forward as a key
Stage 2 adaptation metric, since datasets with very low image availability reduce the meaningfulness
of the multimodal content branch evaluation. Applying the hard criteria to the eighteen datasets listed
in \citet{lv2025multimodal} eliminates datasets that lack network or propagation structure
(e.g.\ COSMOS, MCFEND, Pheme, Fakeddit, Buzzfeed, FakeSv) and datasets using non-compatible
multi-class schemes. The datasets that satisfy the hard acceptance criteria and are therefore carried
forward as Stage 2 candidates are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:stage1_candidates}.

\subsection{Dataset Adaptation}
\label{sec:dataset_adaptation}

Before implementing and evaluating the MACE model, all Stage 2 candidate datasets will undergo
adaptation to verify their suitability for the MACE framework. This assessment checks that each
candidate dataset can support both the multimodal content branch and the social context branch of the
MACE architecture. Adaptation is important to ensure complete modalities within each candidate dataset,
as any missing or incomplete modalities can lower the effectiveness of feature extraction and fusion of
different data types \citep{Baltrusaitis2019}. Dataset candidates will be evaluated based
on the main considerations outlined below.

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Text Availability} \citep{ENGLMEIER2021149}. Text serves as a key source of
    information in misinformation detection. The linguistic, semantic, and contextual clues found in
    posts can strongly indicate authenticity \citep{ENGLMEIER2021149}. In the proposed MACE framework,
    textual content will be processed by the BERT-based encoder adopted from HMCAN to generate semantic
    representations \citep{Qian2021}. Dataset adaptation will therefore assess the availability and
    completeness of textual content to ensure there are sufficient records containing usable text for
    multimodal representation learning and downstream classification.

    \item \textbf{Image Availability} \citep{Qian2021, Dellys2025}. Visual content improves
    misinformation detection performance when combined with textual features. Within the proposed MACE
    framework, images are processed using a ResNet-50 encoder as part of the HMCAN multimodal content
    branch \citep{Qian2021}. Dataset adaptation will evaluate the availability and accessibility of
    image resources, including the proportion of records containing associated images and the presence
    of inaccessible or missing image links, to determine whether sufficient multimodal samples remain
    available for experimentation. This assessment will quantify the ratio of missing or broken media
    links to establish where dummy image placeholders must be introduced to preserve data alignment
    within the HMCAN branch. Datasets with low image availability rates will still be considered for
    inclusion, but the implications for the multimodal branch evaluation will be reported explicitly
    as part of the RQ3 findings.

    \item \textbf{Social-Context Availability} \citep{Song2021}. User interactions, social
    relationships, and information propagation patterns provide evidence for distinguishing authentic
    from inauthentic content. The proposed MACE framework retains the social-context branches of DANES
    and GETAE, which rely on graph-based representations constructed from user networks and propagation
    structures \citep{truica2024danes, GETAE2025}. Candidate datasets must therefore offer sufficient
    user-level metadata, interaction information, or propagation traces to support graph construction
    and social-context modelling. This assessment includes the availability of user records, interaction
    logs, response structures, and network connectivity information.

    \item \textbf{Label Mapping and Class Distribution}. This assessment will verify the mapping of
    disparate source labels (e.g., real/fake vs.\ factual/misinformation vs.\
    rumor/non-rumor/unverified) into a unified binary target classification structure to ensure
    cross-dataset evaluation consistency. It will then evaluate the distribution of target classes and
    measure the degree of imbalance across candidate datasets. An imbalanced dataset can artificially
    inflate accuracy while reducing the model's ability to detect instances of the minority
    class~\citep{Xue2021}. This issue is particularly relevant for misinformation datasets, where the
    distribution of factual and misinformation samples may vary considerably depending on data
    collection and fact-checking procedures~\citep{mumindataset}. For MR2, which provides three
    classes (rumor, non-rumor, unverified), a specific label mapping strategy will be defined and
    reported as part of the adaptation findings.

    \item \textbf{Language and Domain Compatibility}. Candidate datasets collected from non-English
    platforms (Weibo, CHECKED) require assessment of language compatibility with the BERT-based text
    encoder used in HMCAN, which was pre-trained predominantly on English-language corpora. Domain-
    specific datasets (CoAID, ReCOVery) will be assessed for whether their topical focus on COVID-19
    misinformation limits the generalisability of results to the broader misinformation detection
    problem addressed by MACE.

\end{itemize}

As part of this adaptation process, Stage 2 will produce concrete subset statistics for each candidate dataset. These statistics will distinguish the published dataset from the subset that remains usable for MACE after the above adaptation constraints have been evaluated, and be an important feasibility consideration, since the useable adapted dataset must be able to support the controlled ablation conditions required by RQ1 and RQ2.

\subsection{Benchmarking}

To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed MACE model, the results of inauthentic social media
classification from the selected datasets will be compared to previous research works with similar
objectives. The comparisons will be done across three domains: (1) unimodal with social-context
modelling, (2) multimodal without social-context modelling, and (3) multimodal with social-context
modelling similar to the proposed MACE.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Unimodal with social-context modelling}, consisting of DANES \citep{truica2024danes}
    and GETAE \citep{GETAE2025}. These models serve as the primary baseline for combining text or graph
    neural networks with social context features.

    \item \textbf{Multimodal without social-context modelling}, consisting of HMCAN \citep{Qian2021}.
    As a sub-component of MACE, the standalone performance of this model is considered as a comparator
    during ablation experiments.

    \item \textbf{Multimodal with social-context modelling}, consisting of DPSG \citep{jing2025} and
    the multimodal fusion framework of \citet{Dellys2025}.
\end{itemize}

These three domain baselines provide a structured comparison across unimodal and multimodal,
context-aware, and hybrid approaches, ensuring that the performance of the proposed MACE model is
evaluated properly against existing methods in full-model testing and under ablation conditions.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

Based on the methodology described in Section~4, \textbf{Stage 1} forms the baseline for evaluation. \textbf{Stage 2} assesses candidate datasets based on dataset adaptation metrics described in Section~\ref{sec:dataset_adaptation} and these results will be reported as part of the RQ3 findings. 

\textbf{Stage 3} implements and evaluates the MACE framework against the selected dataset or datasets
across several experimental conditions. The main objective is to determine how effective the proposed
model is in detecting inauthentic social media posts. Evaluation adopts standard performance metrics
that are widely used and well-established in fake news detection research \citep{su_wan_liu_huang_2020}.
Prior studies, including DANES \citep{truica2024danes} and GETAE \citep{GETAE2025}, have adopted the
same metrics to assess model performance and enable comparison across different model architectures.
Multimodal research including HMCAN \citep{Qian2021} also utilises these metrics to evaluate the
effectiveness of integrating text and visual features.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Accuracy}, measures the number of correct classifications divided by the total number
    of classifications.

    \item \textbf{Recall}, measures the number of inauthentic posts correctly classified out of all
    actual inauthentic posts (true positive rate).

    \item \textbf{Precision}, measures the number of inauthentic posts correctly classified out of all
    posts classified as inauthentic by the model.

    \item \textbf{F1 Score}, the harmonic mean of recall and precision, balancing false positives and
    false negatives equally.
\end{itemize}

These metrics are compared against the baseline models to assess the effectiveness of the proposed
model \citep{Xue2021}. F1 score will be used as the primary comparison metric across models and
datasets, as it captures the balance of detection capability (recall) and reliability (precision). All
metrics are evaluated during Stage 3 across Conditions A--D, enabling consistent comparison between
the baseline and the modified model and measuring the contribution of multimodal content and social
context features.

Class imbalance is a known issue in misinformation datasets, often arising from the prevalence of false
claims in fact-checking sources~\citep{mumindataset}. To mitigate the impact on evaluation, F1-score
is prioritised over accuracy, and macro-averaged metrics are reported to ensure balanced consideration
of both classes.

To ensure that observed performance differences between models are not due to random variation,
statistical significance testing will be conducted~\citep{stat_test}. For pairwise model comparisons
on classification outputs, McNemar's test~\citep{McNemar_1947} will be used to assess differences in
prediction distributions on the same dataset. For experiments involving repeated runs or
cross-validation, a paired t-test will be applied across folds to evaluate whether differences in
performance metrics (e.g., F1-score) are statistically significant. A significance level of
$\alpha = 0.05$ will be used. Performance will be reported as mean and standard deviation across runs.

\subsection{Computational Efficiency Metrics}

In addition to classification performance, computational efficiency will be evaluated to determine the
practical operational cost of introducing multimodal content processing into the DANES and GETAE
frameworks. Following the framework established by \citet{Justus2018}, a deep learning model's training
and execution cost is heavily affected by factors such as data-loading speeds, hardware memory limits,
and model run-time. The proposed MACE framework's efficiency will be evaluated and reported across the
following computational cost dimensions.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training Time}, measured as the total wall-clock time required to train each
    experimental condition. Mirroring the epoch decomposition strategy of \citet{Justus2018}, this will
    be evaluated as a function of processing time per batch step multiplied by the total step iterations
    required to complete an epoch.

    \item \textbf{Inference Time}, measured as the average prediction time per sample during testing.

    \item \textbf{Peak GPU Memory Usage}, measured as the maximum GPU memory consumed or allocated
    during model training and inference.

    \item \textbf{Peak System RAM Usage}, measured during graph construction and model execution.

    \item \textbf{Storage Requirements}, measured as the total storage required for model checkpoints,
    cached text or image embeddings, and processed graph structural data.

\end{itemize}

These metrics will directly support the evaluation of RQ1 by quantifying the computational cost
associated with substituting the original text-only branch with a multimodal content branch. To ensure
a systematic comparison, these metrics will be logged using the evaluation template structured in
Appendix~\ref{sec:computeexperiment}, Table~\ref{tab:efficiency_matrix}.
