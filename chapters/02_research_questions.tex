\chapter{Research Questions}
\label{sec:research_questions}

This research aims to develop a context-aware misinformation detection framework that replaces the text-only content branch in established ensemble models, such as DANES~\citep{truica2024danes} and GETAE~\citep{GETAE2025}, with a multimodal content branch that jointly captures textual and visual information. Both DANES and GETAE achieve state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets by combining a text content branch with a social context branch that models how information propagates through networks. However, their content branches process only text, ignoring the visual signals that increasingly characterise modern misinformation. While recent work has explored combining multimodal content with social context features~\citep{Nadeem2023, jing2025, Dellys2025}, these approaches employ social branches that differ architecturally from the propagation-graph ensemble structure of the DANES/GETAE family, which use engagement signals, dynamic temporal graphs, or attention-based fusion instead. As a result, two specific research questions remain unanswered, and together they form the technical core of this project.

\paragraph{RQ1.} \textit{How does replacing a text-only content branch with a multimodal content branch affect the performance of context-aware inauthentic social media content detection models, and to what extent?}

\begin{description}
    \item[What it addresses.] This question isolates a single, controlled architectural change: keeping the social context branch of DANES and GETAE intact while substituting the text-only content processor with a content branch that fuses textual and visual features. Specifically, the text-only processor will be replaced with a multimodal component combining a transformer-based text encoder (e.g., BERT) with a CNN- or transformer-based visual encoder for image features. The effect of this substitution will be measured against the original text-only baselines on the same benchmark datasets used in the DANES and GETAE papers, Twitter15 and Twitter16, as well as multimodal FakeNewsNet~\citep{shu2020fakenewsnet} and MuMin~\citep{mumindataset} datasets, using accuracy, F1-score, precision, and recall.

    \item[Significance.] Misinformation on social platforms increasingly relies on manipulated images, memes, out-of-context photographs, and screenshots where the textual signal alone is insufficient or misleading~\citep{Segura2022}. Studies on multimodal-only detectors have shown accuracy improvements of 5--15\% over text-only baselines on datasets such as GossipCop and Fakeddit~\citep{Nakamura2020}. Even strong context-aware ensembles inherit the blindness of their text-only content component, meaning a measurable portion of detectable misinformation may be missed not because the social signal is weak, but because the content signal is incomplete. Quantifying the gain, or absence of gain, from a multimodal substitution directly informs whether the established DANES/GETAE family of architectures is leaving detection accuracy on the table.

    \item[Novelty and difficulty.] No prior work directly performs this controlled substitution and isolates its effect on the DANES/GETAE backbone. Existing multimodal-plus-context models such as EFND~\citep{Nadeem2023}, DPSG~\citep{jing2025}, and the framework of \citet{Dellys2025} use fundamentally different social branches, including engagement signals, dynamic propagation graphs, or attention-based fusion, so their reported results cannot be cleanly attributed to the multimodal component alone. Answering RQ1 requires re-implementing a non-trivial baseline architecture, designing a compatible multimodal branch that integrates with the existing ensemble structure without confounding the social branch, and ensuring the experimental comparison remains controlled across datasets and metrics. This makes the question both technically challenging and methodologically demanding.
\end{description}

\paragraph{RQ2.} \textit{How does the relative contribution of the multimodal content branch and the social context branch affect overall detection performance?}

\begin{description}
    \item[What it addresses.] Where RQ1 asks \emph{whether} multimodal substitution improves detection, RQ2 asks \emph{why} by decomposing where the model's predictive power originates. Through systematic ablation studies, running the content branch alone, the social branch alone, and the full ensemble, the project will quantify how much each branch contributes to correct classification. Additionally, analysis of the fusion-layer weights will reveal whether the two branches provide complementary signals (each capturing different types of misinformation) or whether one branch's contribution is largely redundant when the other is present. This decomposition will be performed across different categories of misinformation to determine whether the balance shifts depending on content type.

    \item[Significance.] Knowing that a combined model performs well in aggregate is not sufficient for either researchers or practitioners. If the social context branch dominates overall performance, then the additional engineering cost of multimodal feature extraction, including image preprocessing, visual model training, and increased inference time, may not be justified for real-world deployment. Conversely, if the multimodal branch dominates, the field should reconsider its current emphasis on increasingly complex propagation-graph architectures and invest more in content-side improvements. The answer therefore has direct design implications for the next generation of misinformation detection systems, informing where computational resources and research effort should be directed.

    \item[Novelty and difficulty.] Most prior multimodal-plus-context studies report only headline accuracy and do not cleanly decompose contributions across a propagation-graph social branch of the DANES/GETAE family. Approaches such as TMEF-BI~\citep{Chen2025} perform reliability-aware modality weighting, but on a fundamentally different social signal (speaker behaviour rather than propagation structure), meaning their findings do not transfer directly to the architecture under investigation. A principled contribution analysis on the proposed backbone is therefore both novel and methodologically demanding, requiring careful experimental design to avoid confounding the two branches and to ensure that observed differences are statistically meaningful rather than artefacts of training variance.
\end{description}

Together, RQ1 and RQ2 form a coherent and complementary investigation: RQ1 establishes \emph{whether} multimodal substitution measurably improves context-aware misinformation detection on the DANES/GETAE backbone, while RQ2 explains \emph{why} by revealing how the content and social context signals interact and contribute to overall performance. Answering both questions is necessary to make a defensible and actionable contribution to the context-aware fake news detection literature.
